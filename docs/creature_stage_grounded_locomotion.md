# Grounded developmental locomotion authority

This additive scaffold replaces pose-cycle translation with physical contact
mechanics before the next neural-motion train. It consumes the approved V7
developmental organisms without altering their genomes, cells, organs,
skeletons, or muscles.

Each grounded frame contains persistent world-space contact anchors, local
limb tethers, skeleton state, node velocity, muscle activation, contact force,
world displacement, and a stable cell correspondence. The 2.5D chassis remains
vertically aligned while the world moves under it; it is never mirrored or
rotated to face travel.

Locomotion is inherited from components rather than hard-coded family labels:

- `leg` components step through alternating stance and recovery phases;
- `root` components creep through long, distributed adhesion phases;
- `wheel` components convert a backward-moving contact patch into hull motion;
- native anomalies translate with a phase field and do not touch the ground;
- non-locomotor arms, fronds, hardpoints, tails, and tendrils remain passive
  with respect to ground reaction.

Consequently, the rooted humanoid uses both step and drag components, and the
muscular anomaly retains ground-capable legs while remaining predominantly
anomalous. These are explicit proofs that family is a prior, not a locomotion
cage.

The deterministic archive is designed as the future loop-v2 teacher and VAE
adapter boundary. It stores all ten reviewed organisms for 72 frames with
cell positions, tissues, trait fields, component diffusion weights, skeleton
state, muscles, contacts, forces, and locomotor-mode IDs. The VAE will consume
the structured living field derived from these arrays; it will not be asked to
invent physics or topology implicitly.

Promotion gates require all five base modes, positive travel, actual traction
work for grounded organisms, zero contact for the native floating anomaly,
contact slip below 0.05 px, edge strain below 0.12, local loop seam below
0.002 px, vertical-axis drift below 5 degrees, exact cell correspondence, and
survival of rare cross-family locomotor grafts. Visual review remains a
separate explicit gate.
